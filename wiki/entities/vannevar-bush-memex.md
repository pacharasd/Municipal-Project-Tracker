# Vannevar Bush (Memex)

> **Type**: Historical Pioneer / Theoretical Device | **Origin**: 1945 ("As We May Think") | **Status**: Canonical Reference

---

## 🏢 Profile & Historical Context
Vannevar Bush (1890–1974) was an American engineer and science administrator who headed the U.S. Office of Scientific Research and Development (OSRD) during World War II. In his seminal 1945 Atlantic Monthly essay *"As We May Think"*, Bush conceived the **Memex** (memory extender): an electromechanical device in which an individual stores books, records, and communications, and consults them with exceeding speed and flexibility.

---

## 🔑 Intellectual Ancestry to LLM Wiki
1. **Associative Trails**: Bush observed that human memory operates by association rather than alphabetical or numerical indexing. The Memex introduced the concept of tying two items together through an associative trail, the direct conceptual forerunner of hypertext and `[[wikilinks]]`.
2. **The Unsolved Problem**: Bush's vision was closer to a private, curated knowledge store than the commercial World Wide Web. However, Bush could not solve the problem of *who maintains the trails and organizes the indices*. In the LLM Wiki pattern, the **LLM Agent** fulfills the maintenance role that mechanical technology in 1945 could not provide.
