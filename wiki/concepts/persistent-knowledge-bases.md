# Persistent Knowledge Bases

> **Domain Category**: Knowledge Engineering | **Status**: Active | **Last Updated**: 2026-09-04

---

## 📖 Definition & Core Principles
A **Persistent Knowledge Base** is a structured, compounding artifact that retains, organizes, and accumulates knowledge over time. Unlike ephemeral query-and-response chatbots or search systems that re-derive information on demand, a persistent knowledge base:
1. Compiles raw input into durable, structured notes.
2. Maintains cross-document relationships through explicit associative trails (`[[wikilinks]]`).
3. Compounds insight: every new piece of information enriches and connects to existing nodes rather than residing in an isolated silo.

---

## ⚙️ The Maintenance Burden & The LLM Breakthrough
Historically, personal wikis and corporate knowledge bases fail for a single structural reason: **the maintenance burden scales faster than user value**. Keeping summaries up-to-date, editing cross-references, tagging entities, and noticing contradictions require immense manual bookkeeping that humans eventually abandon.

By utilizing LLM agents as dedicated maintainers:
- The human focuses on sourcing, curiosity, and critical thought.
- The LLM performs the high-effort bookkeeping, editing dozens of linked files automatically during every ingest.
